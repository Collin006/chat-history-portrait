# Editorial and growth framework

Use this only after the user has calibrated the private portrait. The purpose is to create truthful social objects: material that is enjoyable to recognize, discuss, and share.

## What makes a result travel

Score each candidate angle from 0–3 on six dimensions:

1. **Recognition:** the audience can see themselves or someone they know.
2. **Specificity:** it contains a concrete behavior, choice, or image.
3. **Tension:** it holds two true-seeming ideas in productive conflict.
4. **Surprise:** it reframes familiar behavior without becoming sensational.
5. **Usefulness:** it gives language, a question, or an action to the reader.
6. **Safety:** it works after removing private details and third-party material.

Prioritize angles scoring at least 12/18 with `safety >= 2`. A high total never overrides a low safety score.

## Angle families

Use the material to choose, not force, an angle family:

- **Hidden through-line:** many questions turn out to orbit one deeper concern.
- **Productive contradiction:** two apparently conflicting traits create the person's distinctive method.
- **Change over time:** language, priorities, or decisions shift across periods.
- **Questioning style:** the way the user asks reveals a recognizable working habit.
- **Private operating system:** a repeated process for deciding, learning, building, or recovering.
- **Unfinished chapter:** an honest open question creates forward motion.
- **Counterintuitive strength:** a behavior usually framed as a weakness has a conditional advantage.

Avoid deterministic archetypes, universal praise, fake rarity statistics, “the AI knows you better than you know yourself,” and claims that the full person is captured by the archive.

## Required angle card

Each proposed angle includes:

- title
- one-sentence premise
- opening hook
- why a stranger would care
- approved claim IDs used internally (private planning only; remove from public copy)
- novelty score and resonance score
- privacy risk and the required generalization
- suitable format: profile, carousel, short post, video script, or conversation prompt

## Share-card set

Create a varied set instead of restating one label:

1. **Identity card:** a concise, ownable description.
2. **Contradiction card:** “You are X, but only when Y.”
3. **Timeline card:** a genuine before/after shift.
4. **Question card:** the recurring question underneath many conversations.
5. **Operating-system card:** how the person tends to approach difficult work.
6. **Invitation card:** a prompt others can answer or compare.

Every card needs a strong first line, one central idea, plain language, and a final line that invites reflection rather than begging for engagement. Vary length and rhythm to match the user's voice.

## Public profile structure

Recommended order:

1. a compelling one-sentence portrait
2. three distinctive patterns
3. one productive contradiction
4. one change-over-time observation
5. an unfinished question
6. an explicit scope note: this is a reading of conversation history, not a complete identity

Do not expose confidence scores or evidence machinery in entertainment copy, but preserve honest qualifiers where needed.

## Editorial QA

Reject or rewrite output when:

- it could describe most ambitious or curious people
- the hook depends on a sensitive disclosure
- it overclaims rarity, destiny, intelligence, or psychological truth
- it sounds like a horoscope, résumé, therapy note, or corporate performance review
- all cards are flattering and none contain a genuine tension
- it repeats private wording that a third party could recognize
- the call to action implies official ChatGPT/OpenAI affiliation
